01 / 08 — Intro

Comfortable people complain less.

We all get a little irritable when we're uncomfortable. But what if there was nothing left to complain about?

What if a home simply stayed comfortable, and the family living in it finally ran out of things to grumble about? Nearly.

Let's embrace that idea and make these films feel like beautifully observed family comedies: still, warm and quietly absurd. One cosy living room, one patient camera and a family so content that the only thing left to disturb the peace is each other. And the dog.

And importantly

The comfort should look as good as it feels. Soft furnishings, warm light and a storm the family can barely hear should make this living room feel like the place every viewer wants to be on a cold winter's night.

That balance of stillness, perfect comic timing and a home that genuinely feels better is where these films can make heat pump living feel like a real upgrade.

02 / 08 — Tone & Style

Constant comfort meets comedy.

The tone should feel calm, observational and wonderfully understated.

The comedy comes from the stillness. We treat this family's contentment with complete sincerity, letting the peace build just long enough for something very small, and very human, to break it.

Visually, the world should feel natural and relatable, but with all the craft of premium commercial aesthetics. The living room, the family and even the dog all need the care and attention that will make this a memorable commercial, and one that sits comfortably within the Energy Saving Trust brand world.

Comfort will be expressed through softness. Plush furniture, tactile fabrics, warm ambient light and calm, harmonious colours. A family relaxing in t-shirts while a winter storm rages outside should tell us everything about their heat pump before the voiceover says a word.

And then we contrast all of that with the wonderful, yet sometimes unglamorous, realities of family life.

Each film gives us a different reason why the Johnsons' perfect evening falls apart, but the storytelling approach remains consistent.

A note on the stories

Route 1 and the three Route 2 variations all share the same opening, so I've kept these story sections intentionally light, illustrating the key beats and a few additional moments I see within each film. The tweaks to voiceover, performance and comedy can then be developed together as we move into pre-production.

Route 01 & 02A

Our family aren't just comfortable,

they're blissfully content.

And that's exactly how we'll play this film. A softly lit living room, the TV droning gently and a storm they can barely hear. Using a motion control rig, we naturally transition from the outside environment and into the interior,  letting the camera creep slowly in, we let the peace build until it feels almost too perfect.

The carefully crafted little beats around the voiceover will each have their complementary visual:

01Dad in shorts, toes out, waving at the storm
02Mum, finally free of the dressing gown she's worn since October with a mini fan
03Our son, sprawled on the rug with phone glued to hand and an ice pack on his head
04Our daughter, ice lolly in hand, in the middle of December

These small details will give the performance personality and a light comedic touch that . With no speaking parts, we let the voiceover shine through and complement it visually.

Then, the heat pump

When the dog breaks the silence, we return to the wide to see the collective family response to the disruption. We track backwards to reveal outside, where the heat pump sits quietly beneath the window and, through the glass, we watch the family recoil.

The comedy comes from protecting the peace right up until the last second.

The family genuinely believe they've reached domestic perfection, and we should never undermine that. By keeping the world grounded and the performances beautifully understated, the smallest interruption becomes the biggest joke in the room.

Route 02 · B Nan's Biscuit

This one is all about Nan running out of things to complain about.

I want to embrace Nan's quiet satisfaction.

A gentle push towards her armchair, so we settle in with her.

We'll make this ordinary living room feel like the cosiest place on earth.

The comedy comes from puncturing that serenity with the little moments of family life. Nan's contentment can be built through the framing and the voiceover's gentle list of things she no longer complains about, before a almost flagrant and perfectly timed,

04 / 08 — Characters

The comedy lives in the family.

Casting will be crucial. We need actors with genuine warmth, expressive faces and the timing to say everything without a single line of dialogue.

With no speaking parts, we don't need to force the comedy through them. Their natural reactions combined with the wit of our voiceover is all we need.

The little things will matter most

A sideways glance.
A slow, contented sigh.
A sudden wrinkle of the nose.

The way a family sinks into a sofa when nobody needs a blanket. These details give us the feeling that we're genuinely watching a real home rather than simply putting a voiceover across a cosy set.

The dog

Our dogs should be equally natural. Whether asleep on the rug, eyeing up a biscuit or wedging itself between two parents, the dog shouldn't feel like it's performing a comedy scene. It should simply behave like a dog, which makes the contrast with the family's perfect evening even funnier.

Family

There should also be a real sense of history within this family. We should feel the familiarity between them in the little looks, routines and reactions, so that when the voiceover lists everything they used to argue about, it feels like arguments they've been having for years.

That familiarity gives the comedy something truthful to sit on.

They don't need to act. They just need to be comfortable.

05 / 08 — The Heat Pump

Make comfort something we can see.

The heat pump will always be the quiet hero of our story. It's never the one breaking the silence, and that's exactly the point.

Our art direction, lighting and framing should all work together to make its effect felt in every frame, long before we finally see it beneath the window.

The same level of consideration as
any premium product commercial.

01The comfort

We should feel it in the t-shirts, the bare feet and the relaxed bodies.

02The storm

The weather outside should feel wild enough to make the warmth matter.

03The heat pump

When we finally see it, it should feel neat, modern and quietly reassuring.

Even the environment around the heat pump can reinforce this feeling of quality. A well-kept exterior, warm light spilling from the window and colours that sit naturally within the Energy Saving Trust brand world will create a home that feels aspirational without becoming overly designed.

Everything around the heat pump should support the idea of constant, effortless comfort.

Constant comfort you just can't hide.

06 / 08 — Art Direction & Homes

Comfort through softness.

This living room needs to feel genuinely lived-in and the perfect setting for the family's constant comfort to shine.

Plush furniture with soft, tactile elements layered throughout. Calm, harmonious colours, warm ambient light and organic shapes. A room you'd want to sink into.

Props & dressing
Plush sofas Cushions Unused throws Rugs Family photographs Organic shapes

We can achieve this through carefully selected real props and dressing, all placed with intention. Nothing should feel overly designed or draw attention to itself, but every detail should help tell us something about the family who live there.

Soft, warm and real. With every detail carefully considered.

07 / 08 — Camera & Lighting

Make the home feel irresistible. Make the family feel relatable.

The camera language should be a combination of patient and precise.

Inside, the camera stays almost entirely fixed, starting wide and slowly pushing in so the comfort builds with every second. When the interruption comes, we cut back wide once more, turning the family into a silent tableau before we transition backwards through the glass and reveal the heat pump.

CONNECTIONThe slow push-in
PERSPECTIVEThe feeling of comfort

I love the idea of that single, unhurried push-in, slowly drawing us into the family's world until we feel like we're on the sofa with them. Then we contrast that with the shot of the heat pump beneath the window as the family react behind the glass, exaggerating that familiar feeling of glimpsing a domestic moment through someone else's window.

One room, one camera

Keeping most of the action on a fixed camera in one place lets us communicate the sense and calm and put the time on set where it matters most: the performances, the dressing and the light. Every frame of that push-in can be beautifully composed.

This is where we make comfort look genuinely enviable.

There is something in the patient, fixed-frame stillness that works so well here: carefully composed tableaus where the comedy comes from simply watching. We can attain that level of composure, then soften it with warmth and colour so it never feels cold.

Lighting should feel natural, yet crafted with warm ambient hues that make the living room glow. Lamps, the TV and soft practicals can give the room its cosy, enveloping feel, while the storm outside stays cool, blue and wild, so the contrast between inside and out is impossible to miss.

Warm inside.Wild outside.

08 / 08 — Post-Production

Keep the comedy relateable.

The edit should be calm, confident and rhythmically precise.

The scripts already have strong comic structures, so our job is to give each beat exactly the amount of space it needs to land, while letting the stillness breathe around it. This is how we keep people watching from start to finish.

Contrast

We can use contrast to create much of the rhythm and comedy. A gentle, reassuring voiceover can play over an image of perfect calm, right up until the moment it isn't.

Each film shares the same four-beat rhythm. These beats should feel satisfying and familiar, giving the audience a moment to really appreciate the comfort through crafted and considered visuals, before we take it away.

Music & sound design

Sound design will make these moments even more tactile. A calm, almost zen soundscape lets the comfort settle in, and when the comedy needs to land, a perfectly timed sound breaking through it is exactly what the edit needs.

About the director

Peter Lee Scott writer & director

Peter is a writer-director with a genuine passion for storytelling, memorable performances, VFX, and of course, animals. Winner of Best Director and Best Film at the British Independent Film Festival, producer of a BAFTA-nominated film, and developer of an animated comedy television series with Channel 4 and Objective Productions featuring anthropomorphised cats. 

Peter's stylistic blend of character-driven comedy has led him to direct work for many of the world's biggest brands, including Apple, Diet Coke, Dyson, and animal-focused brands such as ManyPets. Peter's also written and directed comedy commercials for Oatly and Dead Happy Insurance, while winning some of the advertising industry's biggest awards.
